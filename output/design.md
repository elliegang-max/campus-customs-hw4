# Campus Customs — Design Notes

The storefront redesign, and why each change should help shoppers stay and buy.
Front end only; the backend was not touched.

## Look and feel

**Yale-inspired palette + a bold accent.** Deep Yale navy with a gold accent,
defined once as CSS tokens. **Why:** it reads instantly as "Yale gear" and looks
like a real shop, not a demo — the first-impression trust that keeps someone from
bouncing.

**A real font pairing.** Fraunces (display serif) for headings and prices, Inter
for body. **Why:** distinctive and legible; prices set in the display face draw the
eye to exactly what a buyer is deciding on.

## Home

**A strong hero + a stats row** (100+ styles, 14 colleges, XS–XXL, New Haven), with
a gold "Shop the catalogue" button as the one obvious action. **Why:** a visitor
learns what the shop is and takes the next step in seconds instead of scrolling to
figure it out.

**Collection cards** (residential colleges, sports, grad schools, family). **Why:**
most shoppers arrive wanting *their* group; naming those paths gets them to
relevant products faster, which is what turns a browse into a purchase.

## Products

**Polished cards with price up front and stock badges** ("Sold out", "Low stock").
**Why:** scannable cards keep people browsing, and "Low stock" is a gentle nudge to
buy now rather than later.

**Search, sort, and in-stock filtering.** **Why:** with 100+ items, letting a
shopper narrow to "navy under $60, in my size" removes the friction that otherwise
ends in a bounce.

## Single product page

**Premium layout with image zoom and clear price/size/stock.** **Why:** shoppers
can't touch the fabric, so a big zoomable image plus honest per-size stock is what
builds the confidence to add to cart — and prevents the frustration of picking a
size that isn't available.

## Chat assistant

**Branded panel with quick-reply chips, styled bubbles, and a typing animation.**
**Why:** the chips ("What hoodies do you have?") show a hesitant visitor what the
assistant can do and get them a useful, product-linked answer in one tap — turning
a question into a path to the right item. The polish makes it feel trustworthy
enough to actually use.

**Product cards inside the chat.** **Why:** the assistant doesn't just describe
items, it shows them, and each card opens the product page — a direct line from
"what do you have?" to a specific product.

## Motion (used sparingly)

**Hover lifts, fade-ins on scroll, smooth page transitions.** **Why:** subtle
movement makes the site feel modern and cared-for, which supports trust; it's kept
restrained and is fully disabled for anyone who prefers reduced motion, so it never
gets in the way of buying.

## Mobile

**Everything reflows and stays readable on a phone.** **Why:** most campus traffic
is on a phone; a store that's awkward on mobile loses those shoppers outright.
